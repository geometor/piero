On the Five Regular Bodies¶

Libellus de Quinque Corporibus Regularibus — Piero della Francesca (c. 1482–1492)

Author & Autograph Illustrator: Piero della Francesca (c. 1415 – 1492)
Autograph Manuscript: Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Cod. Urb. lat. 632 (f. 1r–68v)
Critical Latin Text: Girolamo Mancini, Memorie della R. Accademia dei Lincei, Ser. V, Vol. XIV (1915), pp. 488–580
1509 Italian Witness: Fra Luca Pacioli, De Divina Proportione, Part III (Venice, 1509, fols. 0085–0136)
Digital Edition: GEOMETOR Scriptorium (Synchronized Latin · 1509 Italian · First Complete English Translation · Exact geometor/polyhedra Commentary)

Composed at Borgo Sansepolcro in the 1480s and dedicated to Guidobaldo da Montefeltro, Duke of Urbino, Piero della Francesca’s Libellus de quinque corporibus regularibus is the foundational Renaissance treatise uniting classical Euclidean stereometry (Books XIII–XV) with abacus radical algebra over \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\).

Epistola

Dedication to Guidobaldo da Montefeltro placing the Libellus beside De prospectiva pingendi.

Treatise I (55 Cases)

f. 2r–21v

pp. 490–516

0085–0100

Triangles, squares, regular pentagon chords & diagonals over \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\), hexagons, and inscribed polygons.

Treatise II (37 Cases)

f. 22r–36v

pp. 517–537

0101–0111

Edges, axes, areas, and volumes of all 5 Regular Solids inscribed in a sphere of diameter \(12\).

Treatise III (29 Cases)

f. 37r–50v

pp. 538–557

0111–0121

Mutual inscriptions among the 5 Platonic solids—featuring Casus 4: Piero’s original inscription of an Icosahedron of edge \(\sqrt{180}-6 = 6/\phi\) inside a Cube of side \(12\).

Treatise IV (19 Cases)

f. 51r–68v

pp. 558–580

0121–0136

5 Truncated Archimedean Solids, 72-faced sphere, 6-edge irregular tetrahedron volume formula (anticipating Cayley–Menger), and exact bicylinder cross-vault volume \(\tfrac{2}{3}d^3\).
